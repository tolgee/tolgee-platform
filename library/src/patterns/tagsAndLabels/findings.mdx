  `Typography`.
</Callout>

## Two names that lie

`TaskLabel` is not a label. It is a grid of an icon and text, 9 call sites, and nothing about it is a
pill. `BranchNameChip` is not a chip — it is `styled(ShieldTick)`, so its props are an icon's props
while what renders is a chip.

<Callout title="Suggested fix">
  Rename `TaskLabel` to what it is. Give `BranchNameChip` a real root and let
  the icon be a child.
</Callout>

## The label color logic is the good part

`globalContext/labelColorUtils` derives the background **and** the text color from one chosen color,
so a label stays readable whatever somebody picks. It is the only place in the product that solves
contrast rather than assuming it.

It is also used by exactly one component. Anything else that lets a person choose a color — and the
language flag editor already does — should use it rather than repeat the calculation.

## Tags and labels are one shape with two meanings

Nothing on screen distinguishes a tag from a label except the color, and an uncolored label looks
exactly like a tag. Both sit on a translation row, both are 24px pills, both are removable.

That is a product question rather than a bug, and it is worth a decision: either labels always carry
their color, or the two need a shape that tells them apart.

## Not wrong

`SuccessChip` works around the missing `success` palette entry deliberately, which is the right thing
to do until **General Findings** is cleared. `TransparentChip` exists for chips on a colored surface —
a real case with a real answer. And the four kinds — tag, label, marker, count — are used correctly
everywhere: nothing in the product makes a marker deletable or a tag colored.
